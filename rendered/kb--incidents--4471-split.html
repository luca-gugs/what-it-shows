How to tell them apart

  • swap: the report shows day and month swapped (“Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July”). A day-first date was read month first. causes.ts splits these into parser and hint by channel and booking form, but the reports do not: they show the same swap, whichever way they came in. One problem, one ticket. The booking-v3 form’s MM/DD/YYYY hint is part of its fix.
  • shift: no swap at all. The date moves by one day: “Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday”, “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” The desk read these as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) but filed them under parser by tenant. Swapping day and month never moves a date by one day, so the date-order fix cannot close them. It looks like a time-zone conversion of the booking date.

Neither the subject line nor the intake tag tells them apart. Read the body.

How the count was made

The desk’s own view, as exported (causes.ts), does the counting:

  1. Take the export of #4471 and keep one report per id. The ticket system exports pages that overlap, so some reports come twice.
  2. Count each report where the desk files it, by tenant, channel and booking form. Filed as parser or as hint, it is swap.
  3. The reports the desk read as a one-day move stay counted where they’re filed, with a footnote. They are also shift, with a ticket of their own.

The numbers on this page are computed from the export when the page renders (#lib/kb/problems.ts), so they follow the data as the desk keeps merging.

What this changes

  • #4471 is not “known, one fix queued”. The queued fix covers swap. shift needs its own fix and its own owner.
  • Merging on the subject alone (decision 0001) cannot keep these apart. That decision stays as it is; a new record should replace it.
